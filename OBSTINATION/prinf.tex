\title{The value of a primitive recursive term at the infinite point\\
{\large a machine-checked computability theorem, from the trace alone}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let $f$ be a primitive recursive term, read as a monotone map on lazy
naturals. We prove, constructively and by a machine-checked argument, that
$f(\Sw,\dots,\Sw)$ is \emph{computable}: a total function produces an
explicit element of the domain, and that element is the least upper bound
of the diagonal chain $f(S^m(\bot),\dots,S^m(\bot))$. The proof is carried
out from a single invariant on the term's \emph{trace} --- the record of
which variable the computation demands at each step and what it has
produced --- rather than from the ultimate-obstination property, which is
not used anywhere and which we note does not follow from the trace
invariant either. We then carry the same analysis to mutual recursion,
where the ultimate-obstination property is outright false but the
trace-level statement is not: a block of simultaneously defined functions
has a trace of the same kind, that trace denotes the block, and for two
functions the verdict follows from the step terms alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{The domain, and primitive recursive terms}

Let $\D$ be the domain of lazy naturals: its elements are
$S^k(\bot)$ for $k \in \N$, $S^k(0)$ for $k \in \N$, and $\Sw$. The order
is
\[
S^j(\bot) \le S^k(\bot) \iff j \le k, \qquad
S^j(\bot) \le S^k(0) \iff j \le k, \qquad
S^j(\bot) \le \Sw ,
\]
and $S^k(0)$, $\Sw$ are maximal. In particular
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:incomp}
S^k(0) \not\le \Sw ,
\end{equation}
the two maximal shapes being incomparable: a computation that has already
produced a $0$ is not an approximation of $\Sw$. Write $\Fin \subset \D$
for the \emph{finite} elements, i.e.\ everything but $\Sw$. Say $x \in \D$
is \emph{incomplete} if it has the form $S^k(\bot)$ (so $\Sw$ is
incomplete but not finite), \emph{complete} if it is some $S^k(0)$, and
write $\hgt(x) = k$ for the number of successors of a finite $x$. Note that
a complete element is maximal among finite ones: if $x$ is complete and
$x \le y$ with $y$ finite, then $y = x$.

A primitive recursive term $q$ of arity $n$ is interpreted on finite
tuples,
\[
\ev(q) : \Fin^{\,n} \longrightarrow \Fin,
\]
by the evident clauses; $\ev(q)$ is monotone. The extension to all of
$\D^n$ is by continuity, and it is that extension we wish to
\emph{evaluate}.

\section{The trace, and the invariant $\MP$}
\label{sec:trace}

\subsection{What a computation looks like from outside}

Take a term $t(x_1,\dots,x_a)$, substitute $\bot$ for every variable, and
ask for its normal form. Either it is already a numeral, or it is
$S^{n_0}(u_0)$ where $u_0$ is stuck: it needs a variable, say $x_{i_0}$.
Replace that variable by $S(x_{i_0})$ --- the least thing that could
unstick it --- and repeat. What comes out is a sequence
\[
(n_0,i_0),\ (n_1,i_1),\ (n_2,i_2),\ \dots
\]
where $n_p$ is the number of successors produced after $p$ substitutions
and $i_p$ is the variable needed next. Equivalently, recording how deep
one has gone in each variable rather than which was taken last, it is a
sequence
\[
\bigl(n_p;\, a_{1p},\dots,a_{ap}\bigr), \qquad a_{1p}+\dots+a_{ap} = p .
\]
This sequence has a \emph{remarkable property}: it determines the value of
$t$ at every finite point at once. For
\[
t\bigl(S^{l_1}(\bot),\dots,S^{l_a}(\bot)\bigr)
\quad\text{has exactly } n_p \text{ successors, where $p$ is
maximal with } a_{ip} \le l_i \text{ for all } i .
\]
One does not re-run the term at each point; one reads off the largest
prefix of the sequence the point can pay for. Everything below is a
consequence of that one observation, applied at increasingly complicated
families of points.

\subsection{The trace}

We record the sequence, and a little more. A \emph{trace} of arity $a$ is
either a leaf $\langle v \rangle$ carrying a value $v \in \Fin$, or a node
consisting of
\begin{itemize}
\item a \emph{walk} $\ivv(0),\ivv(1),\dots$ with $\ivv(k) < a$ --- the
  variable demanded at step $k$;
\item a \emph{value sequence} $\ovv(0),\ovv(1),\dots$ in $\Fin$ --- the
  value the term has when its replay sticks at step $k$;
\item for each coordinate $c < a$ and each $v \in \N$, a
  \emph{continuation}, itself a trace of arity $a-1$.
\end{itemize}
The walk generates its own levels: step $k$ raises coordinate $\ivv(k)$ by
one and leaves the others alone, so with
\[
\lvv(c,0)=0,\qquad
\lvv(c,k{+}1) = \begin{cases}\lvv(c,k)+1 & c = \ivv(k),\\
                             \lvv(c,k) & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\qquad \sum_c \lvv(c,k) = k .
\]
After $k$ steps the walk stands at
$\bigl(S^{\lvv(0,k)}(\bot),\dots\bigr)$, and $\ovv(k)$ is the value there:
the pair $(\ivv,\ovv)$ is exactly the sequence of \S2.1, with $n_p =
\hgt(\ovv(p))$ and $a_{ip} = \lvv(i,p)$. Recording the whole $\ovv(k)$ and
not only its height is essential: a height cannot tell $S^k(0)$ from
$S^k(\bot)$, and $\mathrm{prec}(\mathrm{zerf},\mathrm{zerf})$ --- which is
$\bot$ at $\bot$ and the numeral $0$ above it --- refutes any height-only
version.

\paragraph{Replay.} A trace is evaluated at an actual tuple $X$ of finite
elements by running the walk against the heights $X$ has. Let
\[
\stick(X) \;=\; \text{the first step $k$ at which the walk asks for a
level that $X$ does not have.}
\]
This is a bounded search: the walk spends one level per step, so
$1+\sum_c \hgt(X(c))$ steps of fuel suffice. Writing $k = \stick(X)$,
$i = \ivv(k)$ and $v = \hgt(X(i))$, the value is given by a three-way case:
\[
\sem\langle v\rangle(X) = v, \qquad
\sem(\text{node})(X) =
\begin{cases}
\ovv(k) & \text{$\ovv(k)$ complete,}\\
\ovv(k) & \text{$X(i)$ incomplete,}\\
\sem(\text{continuation at } i, v)\;(X \text{ with } i \text{ deleted})
        & \text{$X(i)$ complete.}
\end{cases}
\]
The three clauses say: the computation has already answered; or it is
blocked on a coordinate that is still $S^j(\bot)$, and $\ovv(k)$ is the
answer; or that coordinate has turned out to be a numeral, in which case
it is frozen into the term and the computation continues at arity $a-1$.
The last clause is why a trace is a tree, and why every argument below
which involves completeness is a recursion on the \emph{arity}.

Two consequences are used constantly. First, \emph{if no coordinate of $X$
is complete the third clause never fires}, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:collapse}
\sem(T)(X) \;=\; \ovv\bigl(\stick(X)\bigr)
\end{equation}
--- the whole semantics collapses to a single lookup in the value
sequence. Second, since a walk spends at most one level per step,
$\lvv(c,k) \le k$; combined with the fact that it sticks exactly when the
level it wants is the one the coordinate does not have, this gives the
dichotomy of \S2.4.

\paragraph{The trace of a term.} The trace is built by induction on the
term, and --- this is the point of building it rather than postulating its
shape --- it is proved to \emph{denote} that term: $\sem(T_q)(X) = \ev(q)(X)$
for every $X$ of the right length. So the trace is not a separate model to
be related to $\ev(q)$ afterwards; it computes $\ev(q)$, and $\ivv,\ovv$
are a decomposition of that computation.

\begin{remark}[provenance, and what this trace is \emph{not}]
\label{rem:prov}
The sequence $(n_p,i_p)$ of \S2.1, together with the observation that it
determines the value of the term at every finite point at once, is the
notion of trace of an email from Coquand to Colson of 1991; the direct
constructive proof of Colson's ultimate obstination theorem that came out
of it is \emph{Une preuve directe du th\'eor\`eme d'ultime obstination},
C.~R. Acad. Sci. S\'er.~I \textbf{314} (1992). The trace of \S2.2 is that
notion, with the whole value $\ovv(k)$ recorded in place of its height
alone, and with continuations added for the coordinates that go complete.

It is \emph{not} the notion of trace of R.~David (\emph{On the asymptotic
behaviour of primitive recursive algorithms}, Theoret. Comput. Sci.
\textbf{266} (2001) 159--193, and \emph{Decidability results for primitive
recursive algorithms}, Theoret. Comput. Sci. \textbf{300} (2003) 477--504, the paper that extends the constructive
result to mutual recursion), although the two are aimed at the same
theorem. There a trace is
a pair $(e,\lambda)$: an element $e$ of the domain \emph{together with a
labelling} assigning to each accessible cell of $e$ a word of tokens $x_n$,
recording which cell of which argument was used to produce that cell. A
term denotes a continuous map from traces to traces, the arguments being
themselves traces. So David's trace is a computation \emph{at a given
tuple of arguments}, decorated with provenance --- in his own reading, a
branch of a Berry--Curien sequential algorithm.

The trace used here carries no provenance and is attached to the term
alone. It is the record of the canonical increasing walk that starts at
$(\bot,\dots,\bot)$ and raises, at each step, exactly the coordinate the
computation is stuck on, together with the value produced at each step; the
value at an arbitrary tuple is recovered by \emph{replay} (\S2.2), not by
reading a label. The two therefore record different things, and neither is
here defined from the other: David's is the finer datum at a fixed point,
since it says which cells were used, while the present one is the finer
datum along the increasing family, since it keeps the whole sequence of
values --- distinguishing $S^k(0)$ from $S^k(\bot)$ --- and descends into a
continuation when a coordinate becomes a numeral.

One derived datum does cross, and it is the one his treatment of mutual
recursion runs on. What that treatment uses of a trace is the static
\emph{calls graph} --- which argument occurrence a recursive call reads
--- which is available to him from the syntax, the call sitting in the
term. Here the demand is not a label but a computation, a function of the
incoming tuple; unwinding it, however, gives
\[
\text{the coordinate demanded at } X \;=\; \ivv\bigl(\stick(X)\bigr),
\]
provided $\ovv(\stick(X))$ and the demanded coordinate of $X$ are both
still incomplete --- the tuple entering only through $\stick(X)$ and
through those two completeness questions. So the demanded coordinate is
the walk's own label; and by clause~(i) of Definition~\ref{def:mp1} that
label is eventually constant, so with $N_i$ its threshold,
$\mathrm{nx}(i) = \ivv_i(N_i)$ is David's $\mathrm{nx}$ --- read off the
invariant rather than off the syntax. This is not a translation of his
traces into these: the labelling of every accessible cell is not
recovered, and the identification holds only past the threshold and while
nothing has completed, which is Colson's obstinate regime. It is enough to
carry his unrolling, and \S\ref{sec:mutual} records that it is also
avoidable.

The theorem has also been proved on the concrete-data-structure side
itself, by P.-L. Curien (\emph{Sequential interactive behaviour of
recursive program schemes}, talk, Tallinn, December 2017): a scheme is
interpreted as a sequential algorithm, and ultimate obstination says that
an infinite branch of it calls infinitely often on exactly one argument.
The proof runs an abstract machine for $\mathrm{rec}(g,h)$ whose states
carry a stack of pending recursive calls, and turns on a \emph{race}
between the outputs a branch of the step term issues and the point at
which it asks for its recursive argument: unless enough outputs have been
issued first, the machine revisits a prefix it has already run, contributes
no new call to any argument, and pushes stacks of unbounded depth --- so
the branch is obstinate with the recursion argument as its index. That
race is the operational form of the verdict clause of
Definition~\ref{def:mp1}: enough successors must be produced, and soon
enough, relative to the demands. The reading is close to David's, as one
would expect of two accounts written in the same model.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The invariant}

\begin{definition}[$\MP$]
\label{def:mp1}
A value sequence $\ovv$ has a \emph{verdict} when
\begin{itemize}
\item either $\ovv(k)$ is complete for some $k$ --- and then, completeness
  being maximal and $\ovv$ monotone, $\ovv$ is constant from $k$ on;
\item or no $\ovv(k)$ is complete, and there is a threshold past which
  $\hgt \circ \ovv$ is either constant or strictly increasing.
\end{itemize}
A trace satisfies $\MP$ when at every node of it
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the walk $\ivv$ is eventually constant, and
\item[(ii)] the value sequence $\ovv$ has a verdict,
\end{enumerate}
and every continuation satisfies $\MP$ as well.
\end{definition}

Clause~(i) is ultimate obstination read along the walk: from some step on
the computation demands one variable and never changes its mind. Clause
(ii) is the three-way shape of the value along that variable.

Two remarks on the exact form. First, the completeness disjunct has to be
kept separate: it is the case where the computation answers, and no
statement about heights can express it. Second, in the incomplete case one
asks for \emph{constant or strictly increasing}, not merely for the
weaker dichotomy ``bounded or unbounded''. The weaker one is what the
theorem of \S3 consumes, but it is not closed under recursion: for a step
whose height may drop back, the orbit's verdict is equivalent to the
limited principle of omniscience. Constant-or-strictly-increasing repairs
this, and one sees why in the recursion clause --- for a strictly
increasing step a single comparison decides the orbit, since $x \mapsto
\varphi(x)$ either fixes a point or moves it upward for ever.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:mp1}
The trace of every primitive recursive term satisfies $\MP$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The engine: the stuck coordinate}

Everything in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:mp1} runs on one observation.
Suppose a trace is fed a family $X_0 \le X_1 \le \dots$ of argument tuples
and let $N_t = \stick(X_t)$, which is monotone. At time $t$ the replay is
stuck at step $N_t$ on the coordinate $d = \ivv(N_t)$, and \emph{the level
it needs there is exactly the height $d$ offers}: the levels a walk has
consumed on the way stay below what was available, and sticking says the
one it wants is not. Hence:
\begin{quote}
\emph{if $d$ never grows again, the replay is stuck at $N_t$ for ever; if
$d$ grows, that is precisely the level it was waiting for, and the replay
advances.}
\end{quote}
There is no third possibility. So $N$ strictly increases until it freezes,
and it freezes for good --- and \emph{which} of the two happens is decided
by the regime of the stuck coordinate, which the induction hypothesis
supplies. Deciding it is moreover a \emph{bounded} search: if $N$ has not
frozen by the threshold of clause (i), then $N$ has passed that threshold,
so from there the stuck coordinate is the single eventual demand, whose
regime is already known.

Given \eqref{eq:collapse}, this settles the value: if the replay freezes,
the value is constant; if it strictly increases, the fed trace's own
verdict transfers along it verbatim. The remaining case is a coordinate
going \emph{complete}, and there the replay freezes too --- a complete
value is maximal, so the coordinate never moves again --- which makes the
continuation descended into a \emph{fixed} smaller trace, and the whole
argument recurses at arity $a-1$. At most $a$ descents happen.

\subsection{Composition}

Let $f(\vec x) = g\bigl(h_1(\vec x),\dots,h_p(\vec x)\bigr)$. The
arguments read the \emph{same} variables, so they cannot be stepped
independently: driving them one at a time charges a shared level twice and
makes the composite stick too early. (Concretely, $f(x) = g(x,x)$ with $g$
the identity in its first argument then answers $S(\bot)$ at $S^2(\bot)$.)
The state must therefore be the composite's own level vector, with each
argument \emph{replayed against it} --- which is exactly the remarkable
property of \S2.1 applied to each $h_i$ at the current $(l_1,\dots,l_a)$.
Sharing is then automatic: raising one level advances every argument that
was waiting for it, at no extra cost.

The composite demands the level that the argument $g$ is currently blocked
on needs; so the selected argument advances by at least one replay step
per stage. That \emph{drive} is what carries an argument's own verdict,
which is stated in its private replay depth, over to the composite's
clock. Together with the fact that the composite's demand is eventually
constant --- say it waits on argument $J$ for ever --- the three cases
of \S2.4 apply with $g$ as the fed trace and the argument values as the
family:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $J$'s value settles, the composite's value is constant, and this
  needs nothing but agreement at the coordinate $g$ is waiting on;
\item if $J$'s value keeps growing, $g$'s replay strictly increases and
  $g$'s own verdict transfers;
\item if $J$'s value becomes a numeral, $g$'s replay freezes, the
  continuation is fixed, and the argument recurses at smaller arity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Primitive recursion}

Let $f(\bot,\vec y) = \bot$ and
$f(S^{j+1}(\bot),\vec y) = g\bigl(S^j(\bot),\,f(S^j(\bot),\vec y),\,\vec y\bigr)$.
Along $f$'s own walk, past the threshold of clause~(i) exactly one
coordinate grows, and there are two shapes.

\paragraph{The recursion argument grows.} Then the parameters are frozen
and $f$'s value sequence is the \emph{chain}
\[
V_0 = \bot, \qquad
V_{j+1} = g\bigl(S^j(\bot),\,V_j,\,\vec y\bigr).
\]
While $V_j$ is incomplete every coordinate of $g$'s argument tuple is
still of the form $S^{\,\cdot}(\bot)$, so \eqref{eq:collapse} applies and
\[
V_{j+1} \;=\; \ovv_g(N_j), \qquad N_j = \text{$g$'s replay depth at depth
$j$} .
\]
This is the situation of \S2.4, and here it has a sharp reading. Writing
$g$'s sequence as $(n_p;\,x_p,y_p,z_p)$ --- $n_p$ successors after $p$
steps, having gone $x_p$ deep in the recursion argument, $y_p$ in the
recursive value, $z_p$ in the parameters --- the criterion is
\begin{quote}
\emph{$f$ loops in its first argument if and only if there is $p$ with
$y(p{+}1) = y(p)+1$ and $n_p \le y_p$,}
\end{quote}
and then $f$'s own sequence is ultimately $(n_p;\,x,z),(n_p;\,x{+}1,z),\dots$
--- constant number of successors --- while otherwise it is essentially
$g$'s. The two halves of the criterion say exactly that step $p$ demands
the recursive value and that the successors produced do not exceed the
depth already consumed of it: that is, that $g$'s replay \emph{stalls} on
the recursive value.

Two facts make this decidable. First, \emph{a stall is never on the
recursion argument}: that coordinate grows by one per depth all by itself,
whereas the levels consumed on the way to the sticking step are below what
was available one depth earlier. Second, a stall on anything else is
\emph{permanent}: a parameter never moves again, and a stall pins the
recursive value, so the coordinate it waits on does not move either. Hence
either the replay strictly increases at every depth --- and $g$'s verdict
transfers --- or it freezes and the height is constant. Which one is
settled by a bounded search, exactly as in \S2.4, and here clause~(i) for
$g$ is what bounds it: past $g$'s own threshold the stalled coordinate
must be $g$'s eventual demand, and each of the three possibilities for
that demand is decided.

\paragraph{A parameter grows.} Then the recursion depth is frozen at some
$c$, and $f$ along the growing parameter is $g$ applied $c$ times over. An
induction on $c$ does it: layer $c+1$ is $g$ fed the family
\[
\bigl(S^{c}(\bot),\ \text{layer } c,\ S^{l+t}(\bot),\dots\bigr),
\]
in which the recursion argument and the frozen parameters never move, the
growing parameter grows at every step, and the middle coordinate is
whatever the induction hypothesis says. That is precisely the hypothesis
of the general lemma of \S2.4, so each layer is one application of it.

\begin{remark}
The direction is worth stating explicitly, because an earlier version of
this development went the other way. The trace argument above does
\emph{not} consume the ultimate-obstination property; it is proved from
the trace by induction on the term, and the ultimate-obstination property
is not used anywhere in it.
\end{remark}

\section{The theorem}

Fix an arity $n$ and write
\[
\Delta_m \;=\; (S^m(\bot),\dots,S^m(\bot)) \in \Fin^{\,n},
\qquad
\Delta_\infty \;=\; (\Sw,\dots,\Sw) \in \D^n .
\]
The chain $c_m := \ev(q)(\Delta_m)$ is monotone in $m$ by monotonicity of
$\ev(q)$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:main}
For every primitive recursive $q$ of arity $n$ one can compute an element
$\val(q) \in \D$ which is the least upper bound of $(c_m)_{m\in\N}$. In
particular $q \mapsto \val(q)$ is total, so $f(\Sw,\dots,\Sw)$ is
computable, uniformly in $q$.
\end{theorem}

The all-infinite point is the \emph{easy} point, and the proof is three
lines of the machinery of \S2.

\begin{lemma}[the chain is a lookup]
\label{lem:lookup}
$c_m = \ovv(N_m)$, where $N_m$ is the replay depth of the trace against
the level vector $(m,\dots,m)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
No coordinate of $\Delta_m$ is complete, so \eqref{eq:collapse} applies;
and the trace denotes $\ev(q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the lookup is cofinal]
\label{lem:cofinal}
$N_m \ge m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The walk spends at most one level per step, so at step $k < m$ it has
consumed at most $k < m$ levels of the coordinate it is about to demand,
and $m$ are available. Hence it does not stick before step $m$.
\end{proof}

So the chain, reindexed, runs through the value sequence cofinally, and
its least upper bound is the least upper bound of $\ovv$. The verdict of
Definition~\ref{def:mp1} names that:
\[
\val(q) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\ovv(k) & \text{$\ovv$ complete at $k$ (a numeral),}\\
S^{\,\hgt(\ovv(k))}(\bot) & \text{$\ovv$ never complete, height constant from $k$,}\\
\Sw & \text{$\ovv$ never complete, height strictly increasing from $k$.}
\end{cases}
\]

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
\emph{Complete at $k$.} Then $\ovv$ is constant from $k$ on. For any $m$,
$\ovv(N_m) \le \ovv(k)$: either $N_m \le k$ and this is monotonicity, or
$N_m \ge k$ and the two are equal. So $\ovv(k)$ is an upper bound, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cofinal} it is attained at $m = k$, hence least.

\emph{Never complete, height constant from $k$.} Every $c_m$ is
$S^{\hgt(\ovv(N_m))}(\bot)$, and $\hgt(\ovv(N_m)) \le \hgt(\ovv(k))$ by the
same split. Attained at $m=k$, again by Lemma~\ref{lem:cofinal}.

\emph{Never complete, height strictly increasing from $k$.} Each $c_m$ is
incomplete, hence below $\Sw$, so $\Sw$ is an upper bound. It is the least
one: a strictly increasing height is unbounded, so for any finite $d$ ---
of either shape --- Lemma~\ref{lem:cofinal} produces an $m$ with
$\hgt(c_m) > \hgt(d)$, and $d$ is not an upper bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Only Lemma~\ref{lem:lookup} uses that \emph{every} coordinate of the point
is infinite, and it uses only that none is complete and none is finite ---
so nothing descends and nothing blocks. That is the whole reason the
argument at $\Delta_\infty$ is so much shorter than at a general point.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what $\MP$ does not give]
\label{rem:notuo}
It does \emph{not} give the ultimate-obstination property. The leaf trace
$\langle S^0(\bot)\rangle$ of arity one satisfies $\MP$ vacuously and
denotes the constant $\bot$, whose ultimate-obstination property fails at
the point $(0)$: at an all-complete point the property leaves no room ---
its second case wants a coordinate that is incomplete and finite, its
third wants one that is $\Sw$ --- so its first case must hold and the
value must be a numeral, which a constant $S^m(\bot)$ is not. The missing
ingredient is \emph{totality}, that a term applied to numerals returns a
numeral; that is a separate (easy) induction on the term, and it is not
needed for Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{remark}

\section{The formalisation}

All of the above is formalised in Agda and checked with
\verb|--safe --without-K --exact-split|, with no postulate, no hole and no
termination pragma.

\begin{itemize}
\item The trace, its semantics and its levels, and the proof that the
  trace of a term denotes the term: \texttt{TraceDef}, \texttt{ReplayLv},
  \texttt{TrSat}, \texttt{TrDen}, \texttt{TrTerm}. The collapse
  \eqref{eq:collapse} is \texttt{TrSat.sem-bot}.
\item The invariant and its two clauses: \texttt{TrMP1}. Composition:
  \texttt{TrComp}, \texttt{TrCompNG}, \texttt{TrCompSel},

  Recursion: \texttt{TrPrec}, \texttt{TrPrecIvPMP} for the index clause,
  and \texttt{TrPrecChain}, \texttt{TrPrecStall}, \texttt{TrPrecPhi},
  \texttt{TrPrecParPhi}, \texttt{TrPrecOvP} for the value clause. The
  general lemma of \S2.4 is \texttt{TrFeedR} (with

  in advance to settle).
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:mp1} is \texttt{TrTermMP1}, and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is \texttt{PRInfMP1}. Neither uses the

The proof \emph{runs}: a companion file checks by evaluation that
\[
\begin{array}{lcl}
\mathrm{zerf}(\Sw) &=& 0,\\
\mathrm{proj}_0(\Sw) \;=\; \mathrm{succ}(\Sw) &=& \Sw,\\
E(\Sw) &=& 0 \quad\text{where } E = \mathrm{prec}(\mathrm{zerf},\mathrm{zerf}).
\end{array}
\]
$E$ is the informative one: $E(\bot) = \bot$ but $E(S^{m+1}(\bot)) = 0$,
so the chain is $\bot, 0, 0, \dots$ and the least upper bound is a
numeral, at a point that is infinite.

\section{Mutual recursion}
\label{sec:mutual}

Consider now a block of $r$ functions defined by simultaneous primitive
recursion,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:block}
f_j(S(x),\vec y) \;=\; h_j\bigl(x,\; f_1(x,\vec y),\dots,f_r(x,\vec y),\;
\vec y\bigr), \qquad 1 \le j \le r,
\end{equation}
with the $h_j$ already given.

For such a block the ultimate-obstination property is \emph{false}: there
is a two-function block whose value along the diagonal has height
$\lfloor m/2 \rfloor$, which is neither constant nor strictly increasing,
so the shape the property's third case demands cannot be met. This does
not touch Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, which is proved from the trace and $\MP$
alone and nowhere from that property; and $\lfloor m/2 \rfloor$ is
unbounded, so the value of that very block at $\Delta_\infty$ is $\Sw$,
and is computed. What a block needs is $\MP$, not ultimate obstination.

Sections \ref{sec:trace}.4--2.6 transfer unchanged: they are about a trace
fed a monotone family, and nothing in them mentions how the function was
defined. The stuck-coordinate dichotomy, the bounded search that decides
it, and the descent at a coordinate that goes complete therefore apply to
a block's step terms as they stand, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:lookup} and
\ref{lem:cofinal} are facts about $\Delta_\infty$ and the order on $\D$.

\paragraph{The trace of a block.} A block has a trace of exactly the kind
of \S2.2: it is the trace of primitive recursion with its layer a vector,
the block unfolding on the numeral depth rather than on a subterm. It
carries value sequences and continuations as a single term's trace does,
and, like it, is proved to denote what it is the trace of --- its
semantics is the block's evaluation, component by component. So nothing in
\S\ref{sec:trace} has to be restated for blocks; only the object it is
applied to changes.

Recording values rather than heights is what makes that true. An account
of the same dynamics that keeps only how tall each component is at each
unfolding stays correct as long as no component has answered, and fails as
soon as one does: for $f_1 = 0$ and $f_2 = f_1 + S(f_2)$ the real $f_2$
climbs by one per unfolding, so its value is $\Sw$, while the height-only
dynamics freezes it at $\bot$. The reason is the third clause of the
semantics of \S2.2 --- a coordinate that has gone complete makes the
computation descend into a continuation --- and a height cannot see a
descent.

\paragraph{Step terms, not arbitrary traces.} The invariant does not
transfer for arbitrary traces. There is a pair of traces satisfying the
corresponding hypotheses for which the bare bounded-or-unbounded
dichotomy for the block implies the limited principle of omniscience;
classically the same point reads that ``is the height of $f(S^m(\bot))$
bounded in $m$?'' is $\Pi^0_1$-hard. That example does not come from a
block of terms. Its step height is $b(n)+n$ for an arbitrary binary $b$,
and what makes the orbit hard is exactly that $b$ may drop from $1$ to
$0$; a step term's height is constant or strictly increasing past a
threshold that the proof of $\MP$ produces, and $b(n)+n$ admits such a
threshold only when $b$ never drops after it --- which already decides the
principle. So closure fails over arbitrary traces, and an argument for a
block must use the step terms; no primitive recursive block is exhibited
whose value at $\Delta_\infty$ is uncomputable.

\paragraph{Two functions.} For $r = 2$ the verdict for the block follows
from the invariants its step terms already carry, with no obstinacy
assumed anywhere. Each component is decided separately to be obstinate or
to answer, and decided from a supremum that is \emph{attained} rather than
merely bounded --- a bound on the replay depth does not say whether a
component ever answers. A component that has answered is fixed for ever,
so the block descends to a system of the same shape at one lower arity,
and the same argument applies to it.

\paragraph{The unrolling, and David's calls graph.} A block's depth
sequence is the orbit of a single map, and there are two ways to say so,
differing exactly in whether the calls graph of Remark~\ref{rem:prov} is
used.

Substituting into the one coordinate that graph names gives David's unary
$\Phi_i$, and with it his iteration law: the value after $n+p$ unfoldings
is $\Phi_i$ applied to the value after $n$. What that costs is the
hypothesis that the step term really is blocked at the named coordinate,
which is the conjunction of the two obstinacy conditions of
Remark~\ref{rem:prov} with the requirement that the walk has passed its
threshold. The last is not free: a step term stuck on a parameter does not
advance, and so does not reach it.

Filling \emph{every} recursive coordinate at once removes the need for it.
Advancing the depth coordinate by $t$ successors --- David's shift
$x \mapsto x+p$, here literally $S$ composed $t$ times --- the tuple the
step term receives is the block's own $t$-th unfolding, coordinate by
coordinate; so there is no saturation step and no demand to control, and
the fixpoint law holds with nothing assumed at all. What is traded away is
that the iteration is then a map on vectors of length $r$ and not a unary
one. Free and $r$-ary, or unary and conditional: that is the choice, and
it is the only place in the block argument where the calls graph does any
work.

\paragraph{The value of a block at the infinite point.} The level-by-level
verdict holds for blocks unconditionally: for every $K$ one can decide
whether the value reaches $S^K$, because a component's height past its
threshold is a deterministic monotone iteration, decided by $K+1$ steps.
So the value of a block at the infinite point is computable \emph{as a
lazy natural}, which is the constructively correct reading of such an
object. Producing it instead as an element of the three-constructor
datatype $\D$ --- which builds the bounded-or-unbounded dichotomy in,
$S^k(\bot)$ and $\Sw$ being different constructors --- is what the trace
of a block gives, for two functions.
