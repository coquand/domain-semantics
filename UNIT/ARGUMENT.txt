Section 2 in Unit: checked proof architecture
============================================

1. Keep the finite MIN proofs and change the completion.

The finite core is copied unchanged into vendor/. Its original filters
retain membership witnesses. ExtFilter makes membership a proposition
using replacement-derived truncation. Univalence then turns mutual
inclusion into identity. No witnesses are chosen from truncation.

Truncation alone is insufficient. Unrestricted consistent MIN filters can
contain arrows with inconsistent inputs, undetectable by application to
a consistent argument. Proper restricts arrow inputs to consistent proper
tokens and Pi families to proper arrow graphs. Induction on Tp proves
typed-rep, so this restriction loses no well-typed MIN observations.

2. Build the domain and its actual function space.

D consists of consistent filters generated by proper tokens. Directed
suprema are unions. Bounded suprema are generated by finite meets of
observations from the bounded family. Principal proper consistent filters
are compact, and every element is a directed union of these.

Application reflects MIN application. Abstraction collects finite arrow
graphs observed by a continuous map on compact inputs. Consistency follows
from monotonicity and consistency of its values. Properness lets eta test
every arrow at its principal input. Beta uses finite character. Continuous
also proves the equivalence with directed-supremum preservation.

MIN's consistency predicates are proof-relevant. ConsStable first proves
stability of bottom comparisons, then of fuel-indexed consistency. This
recovers Cons from Ex(Cons) without general untruncation or choice.

3. Decode and reconstruct the coalesced sum.

A filter records positive U/Pi head propositions, conditional Pi parts,
and its function component. The heads are disjoint; either head forces
the function component to bottom. Lifting is a proposition together with
a value defined under it, without decidable definedness.

Sum encodes this data, Equation proves both inverse identities, and
EquationOrder proves monotonicity in both directions. Together with the
function-space equivalence these give the domain equation.

Minimal proves the fixed-point/minimality statement directly. Its mutual
induction handles domain and arrow-family observations. The arrow case
uses its input/output hypotheses, and Pi uses its domain/graph hypotheses.
This avoids rebuilding a separate rank calculus.

4. Represent projectors by their compact fixed points.

A compact family contains bottom, is closed under consistent finite joins,
and respects token equivalence. Its projector retains the observations
generated by family members already present in its input. This proves
continuity, deflation, idempotence and the compact fixed-point description.

Finitary uses compact fixed-point density as its image-algebraicity
condition. Reconstruction recovers every such projector from its family.
FixedImage constructs the image's suprema and compact basis, and proves
compactness agrees with ambient compactness. ProjectorDomain constructs
small joins, directed unions, a basis of finite generators, and pointwise
directed suprema of the induced projectors.

5. Connect to MIN without rebuilding stage-indexed typing.

SemanticProjector closes the typed compact generators of a type filter,
giving a continuous interpretation D -> Fp(D). Compare proves agreement
with projF on consistent regular raw inputs. Normalizing typed generators
using Proper.typed-rep is the key reverse step. Model proves projector
fixedness for well-typed denotations and identity for conversion.
LogicalRelation transports adequacy to mere observations; original
untruncated adequacy and its syntactic consequences remain available.

Stages provides a separate approximation theorem: close compact tokens
of depth <= n under compatible joins/equivalence and take projector images.
The chain starts at bottom and exhausts D. Inclusions and restrictions are
embedding-projection pairs. This does not prove the paper's literal
successor-stage equation or stage-indexed typing diagrams; those are
bypassed by the global construction.

6. The recursive equations and leastness (subsequent extension).

SemanticLaws proves the typed compact generators already form a family,
and proves normalization p(p(U,A),X) = p(A,X). ProjectorEquations proves
both nontrivial recursive clauses for arbitrary domain arguments; zero
cases are in ProjectorZero. All finite witnesses are combined inside
propositional truncation.

ProjectorLeast proves, by simultaneous element/entry typing induction,
that p lies below every continuous binary map satisfying the three lower
clauses. No projector laws of the competing map are assumed.
ProjectorFixedPoint packages existence of a least recursive solution and
derives finitariness for every such solution by identification. SECTION24
explains the Kleene construction and its simultaneous invariants. The
explicit iteration is now encoded in KleeneProjector; finitariness of its
limit is proved by identification. The invariant proof for each iterate
remains informal.

These claims have checked declarations. README.txt gives exact theorem
names, assumptions, universes, and scope differences from the paper.

7. General Scott domains and Kleene fixed points.

ScottOrder and ScottDomain give a general universe-aware interface with
small order, small compact basis, bottom, and small directed and bounded
suprema. ScottMaps constructs pointwise suprema of continuous maps.
ScottExponential proves algebraicity using finite compatible step graphs,
and characterizes all compact maps this way. ScottFilter instantiates
the interface and identifies the generic maps with Continuous.Map.
ScottFixpoint proves the general Kleene fixed-point and leastness theorem.

ProjectorOperator defines the actual guarded recursive functional and
proves monotonicity. OperatorContinuity now proves joint continuity, and
BinarySup and KleeneProjector construct its pointwise Kleene least fixed
point. SCOTT.txt gives the function-space proof and explains
the finite compatibility condition, which is stronger than the paper's
pairwise criterion for arbitrary Scott domains.

8. The printed stage-zero convention needs correction.

StageBaseObstruction retains the stage dependencies of Figure 2 and
weakens the other Pi/Lam premises. Even this permissive relation has no
stage-zero derivations and cannot type Pi(bottom,bottom) at stage one.
An independent stage projector generated from these typings sends that
element to bottom, whereas restriction of the global projector retains
it. The inequality is checked. PROPOSITION227.txt explains the scope of
this formal obstruction. The full paper stage construction has not been
claimed as formalised.

9. Corrected rank-stage compatibility.

CorrectedRank starts with rank(bottom) = rank(U) = 0. CorrectedStages
constructs the projections onto the rank-bounded compact ideals, their
embedding-projection pairs, and exhaustion of D. StageInterpolation
proves the key two-sided lemma: given L <= u <= X and b <= A with u:b
and bounded L,X,A, replace u,b by bounded typed witnesses in the same
interval and below the same type cap. Neither new witness is required
to lie below the original one.

StageCompatibility independently generates p_n from bounded MIN typings.
Interpolation proves that every observation of the restricted global
projector occurs in p_n; inclusion of typed generators proves the converse.
KleeneProjector.identification transfers this to the independently iterated
global p. proposition-2-27 is an identity in D_n for every n. This is the
corrected MIN rank-stage theorem; a comparison with a separately encoded
Figure 2 relation and a successor-stage functor isomorphism remain outside
its scope. PROPOSITION227.txt records the exact definitions.

10. Direct finite-unfolding proof of stage compatibility.

RankProjection identifies q_(n+1) with the structural action of q_n on
the domain equation. For a stage-(n+1) element, its application function
takes stage-n values and factors through q_n; the same holds for its Pi
family, and its Pi domain lies in stage n.

Consequently StageFusion proves: if two binary operations agree after
stage-n restriction on stage-n arguments, one application of Phi makes
them agree after stage-(n+1) restriction on stage-(n+1) arguments. At
stage zero, Phi's universe head is independent of the input operation.
Induction on n gives stabilization of the restricted Kleene sequence at
P_(n+1). OperationalStages defines its stage operations by these finite
unfoldings and passes to the supremum to prove 2.27. These modules are
checked before StageInterpolation is imported and use no typing-projector
identification.

StageComparison separately identifies the operational and bounded-typing
stage operations. It transfers finitariness to the operational ambient
extensions through the earlier sandwich proof. Independent finitariness
of every Kleene iterate remains outside the checked direct proof.

ScottStepApprox supplies the direct function-space approximation proof:
construct compact steps by bounded completeness, form finite joins under
a given upper function, and prove their directed density. It is checked
before ScottExponential and uses no graph-selection machinery. The older
admissible presentation still supplies the small global compact basis.
