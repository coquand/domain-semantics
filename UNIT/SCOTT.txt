General Scott domains and their function spaces
===============================================

Checked interface
-----------------

ScottDomain.Scott describes a pointed, bounded-complete algebraic dcpo
with a small compact basis. The carrier is in U (l+); the order and basis
indices are in U l. Directed and bounded completeness are for families
indexed by U l. Order is propositional and antisymmetric with respect to
identity. The carrier is consequently a set (ScottOrder.On.order-set).

ScottMaps.Between[D,E].Map consists of monotone functions preserving
small directed suprema. The preservation field says that every upper
bound of the image family is above the image of its supremum. Together
with monotonicity this is exactly the usual supremum equation.

The order on maps tests only the input compact basis. ScottMaps.pointwise
proves equivalence with the full pointwise order, keeping the order small.
Pointwise suprema are continuous, for both directed and bounded families.

The main theorem is:

  ScottExponential.Exponential[D,E].scott : ScottDomain.Scott

Its carrier is exactly ScottMaps.Between[D,E].Map, with no extra hypotheses
on D or E. Its bottom is the constant bottom function. Its compact basis
is the small type of admissible finite graphs of input/output basis pairs.
compact-graph proves that every compact continuous map is represented by
such a graph, propositionally and up to equality of functions.

The direct finite-join proof
----------------------------

ScottStepApprox is now a separate checked proof, imported before
ScottExponential. It uses no graph selections or admissibility condition.
The mathematical presentation in scott-domains.tex follows this proof.

For compact a,b the step a => b is constructed by bounded completeness,
as the supremum of the constant b family indexed by a <= x. Step.below
and Step.test prove (a => b) <= f iff b <= f(a). Step.compact proves
compactness using compactness of b at the input a.

Given a continuous f and a finite list (a_i,b_i) with b_i <= f(a_i),
all the steps are bounded by f. FiniteJoin therefore forms their join
directly using pointwise bounded completeness. Its compactness follows
by combining finitely many directed witnesses. This construction never
needs to decide which entries are active at an input.

Approximation[f] takes all such finite lists. It is a small directed
index type: the empty list gives bottom and concatenation gives a common
upper approximation. Compact-input witnesses from continuity show that
the singleton steps already cover every observation of f. equation proves
that the supremum of the finite joins is f; compact-finite proves that a
compact f is itself one finite join.

The small global presentation
-----------------------------

The older ScottExponential construction additionally supplies a single
small basis index type, uniformly for all functions. It uses intrinsic
boundedness of finite presentations to avoid a large existential over
upper functions. This representation is separate from the direct
finite-join approximation argument above.

For compact a in D and b in E, the step function a => b is the supremum
of the constant b family indexed by the proposition a <= x, at each x.
It does not require deciding this proposition. Its characteristic law is

  (a => b) <= f    iff    b <= f(a).

The formal declarations are step-below and step-test. Compactness of b,
and pointwise directed suprema, imply compactness of this step function.
The same argument, combining finitely many directed witnesses, proves
compactness of every admissible finite graph.

A finite graph G is admissible when every finite selection of its entries
whose input compacts have a common upper bound has output compacts with a
common upper bound. Bounds can be tested using basis elements: finitely
many compacts below x have a common basis upper bound below x. Thus the
admissibility predicate is U l-valued.

To evaluate G at x, consider finite selections whose inputs are all below
x. Each selection has a bounded output join. These joins form a small
directed family, under concatenation of selections. Its supremum is G(x).
This avoids treating the undecidable active part of a finite graph as a
decidable finite set. The key universal property is

  G <= f    iff    b_i <= f(a_i) for every entry (a_i,b_i) of G.

Every finite graph satisfying the right side is admissible. Finite unions
of graphs below f remain below f, so these graphs form a directed family.
Continuity of f gives a compact input witness for each compact output
observation. The corresponding singleton graph lies below f and covers
that observation. Hence f is the supremum of the compact graphs below it.

No choices are extracted from mere bounds. A bounded family's supremum,
together with its universal property, is a proposition by antisymmetry.
Truncation can therefore eliminate a mere bound into this unique result.

A correction to the general pairwise criterion
----------------------------------------------

The paragraph following Definition 2.11 in paper1.pdf gives a pairwise
compatibility criterion for finite step presentations. For general Scott
domains, pairwise compatibility alone does not suffice.

For example, let D be the one-point domain and let E be the poset of all
proper subsets of {1,2,3}, ordered by inclusion. E is a finite pointed
algebraic dcpo and is bounded complete: a bounded family's union is still
a proper subset. The three singleton subsets are pairwise compatible,
but have no common upper bound. The graph sending D's single point to
each of these three singleton outputs satisfies the pairwise criterion
yet cannot define a function value there.

The finite-selection condition above is necessary and sufficient and
needs no extra coherence assumption. This counterexample is an informal
observation; the checked theorem itself uses finite-selection admissibility.

Connection to the existing construction
---------------------------------------

ScottFilter.scott packages the existing intersection-type filter domain.
ScottFilter.map-equivalence identifies the generic continuous maps with
Continuous.Map, preserving the underlying functions in both directions.
ScottFilter.function-scott instantiates the general exponential theorem.

ScottFixpoint.Kleene[S,f] defines f^n(bottom), proves that this is directed,
and takes its supremum. equation proves f(value) = value; Below.least proves
value <= x whenever f(x) <= x. No projector property is assumed.

The projector-specific functional is defined in ProjectorOperator.At.
OperatorContinuity now proves its joint directed continuity, and
KleeneProjector explicitly constructs its Kleene least fixed point,
identifies it with the typed projector and derives finitariness and
normalization. RankProjection, StageFusion and OperationalStages now
give a direct finite-unfolding proof of stage compatibility: stage-n
observations of the Kleene sequence stabilize at P_(n+1). This proof
does not use typing identification or the sandwich lemma. StageComparison
separately identifies the operational and bounded-typing stages and
transfers finitariness. PROPOSITION227.txt explains the two routes and
the base-stage obstruction to the unamended printed construction.

All proofs use only the existing univalence and replacement parameters;
there is no excluded middle, choice or propositional resizing assumption.
